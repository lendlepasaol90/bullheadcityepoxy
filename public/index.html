A floor company that plans its day around the sun

Bullhead City sits low on the Colorado River, a few hundred feet above sea level, across the water from Laughlin. That low elevation gives the town its mild winters and its punishing summers. It also shapes every floor we put down. Concrete here can sit far hotter than the air in the afternoon, and liquid coatings respond to slab temperature more than anything else.

So our work starts early and stops when the slab says so. We grind and repair the concrete first, test it for moisture, pick products that tolerate the conditions, and spread resin when it will level and bond the way it should. The result is a floor that looks good on day one and still looks good after a few summers of hot tires, river sand and open garage doors.

We coat garages, casitas and living spaces, patios and pool decks, shops, restaurants, warehouses and fleet buildings across Bullhead City, Fort Mohave, Mohave Valley and the towns up the hill toward Kingman.

Why homeowners and businesses call us

Garage floors for boats, RVs, side-by-sides and the family car

In a river town, the garage does a lot more than hold a sedan. It is where the boat waits on its trailer, where the jet skis get rinsed off, where the side-by-side comes back caked in wash sand, and where an RV bay might take up half the lot. Those floors see fuel, two-stroke oil, wet sand and heavy point loads from trailer jacks and stabilizers.

Our garage floor coatings start with grinding and crack repair, then build a base coat, a full or partial broadcast of color flake and a UV-stable topcoat. In tall RV and boat bays we add thickness under jacks and leveling blocks. Around the door we can add texture, because wet floors and flip-flops are a poor mix.

Living spaces, casitas and laundry rooms

Plenty of homes here are built on slab, and many owners are tired of carpet that holds dust or tile with grout lines that never look clean. A coated floor is seamless, cool underfoot and easy to mop. It suits casitas, game rooms, laundry and mudrooms, and full living areas in homes that go for a modern look.

Residential epoxy inside the house is about patience: careful dust control, flattening the slab, a moisture check, and finishes that suit the room. Solid colors, fine flake, quartz textures and polished-look clear coats are all on the table. For a showpiece room, metallic epoxy gives the floor movement and depth.

Commercial floors for shops, restaurants and service businesses

The local economy runs on visitors, retirees and the people who look after them, and that means a lot of floor traffic. Restaurants and bars need surfaces that shrug off spills and hot cleaning water. Clinics and offices want something bright and simple to sanitize. Retail shops and showrooms want a floor that looks good with sand tracked in from the parking lot.

Our commercial epoxy flooring is scheduled to keep you open, often in sections or over a closed day. We match the system to the business: slip-resistant textures in kitchens, chemical-resistant topcoats in service areas and decorative finishes in customer-facing rooms.

Warehouses, fleet yards and industrial buildings

Along Highway 95 and up toward Kingman there are distribution buildings, marine and RV repair shops, contractor yards and equipment storage that take a beating. Forklifts, pallet jacks, hydraulic fluid and grit all wear concrete down, and dusty floors end up on stock and in machinery.

Our warehouse floor coatings focus on durability: heavy grinding, joint repair, high-build epoxy or flexible polyurea bases, chemical-resistant topcoats and painted-in lanes and safety zones. We work in phases so racking and operations can shift around us.

Decorative and metallic finishes

Coatings do not have to look industrial. Decorative flake floors use layered vinyl chips to give a stone-like look that hides dust and sand between sweeps. Blends can lean warm, with tan and copper, or cool, with grey and river blue.

Metallic epoxy goes further. Pigments are worked by hand through clear resin to create swirls, veins and soft clouds. It suits living rooms, home bars, salons and showrooms, anywhere the floor is meant to be looked at as well as walked on.

How a project runs, start to finish

Five stages, the same on a single-car garage as on a warehouse.

  1. 1

    Look and measure

    We visit, measure, check moisture, find old sealers and talk about how you use the space.

  2. 2

    Written quote

    You get an itemized proposal with the system we recommend and why.

  3. 3

    Grind and repair

    Diamond grinding with dust extraction, then crack, pit and joint repair.

  4. 4

    Coat in the cool hours

    Base, broadcast or pigment and topcoat, timed to the slab temperature.

  5. 5

    Walk-through

    We check the floor with you and explain cure times and care.

Preparation carries most of the weight. A coating is only as strong as the concrete surface it grips, and builder sealers, paint, curing compounds, efflorescence and oil stains all get in the way. Grinding removes them and leaves a texture the base coat can lock into. Cracks are cut out and filled, spalled edges rebuilt, and control joints either honored or filled depending on where they are.

After that, timing is everything. A base coat that goes down on a cool slab has time to soak in and level. The same coat on a sun-baked slab can kick off in the bucket. That is why our summer start times look unusual, and why we sometimes recommend fast-curing polyaspartic systems that finish in a single day.

Why Bullhead City owners choose a coated floor

A lot of people here split their year between the river and somewhere cooler, and they want a home that is easy to lock up and come back to. A sealed garage and patio mean less dust, fewer stains and nothing that needs scrubbing on arrival.

Others are here full time and live outdoors as much as the weather allows. Their garages double as workshops, gyms and gear rooms. Their patios host family all winter. They want floors that look finished and take abuse.

Business owners see the same thing at a larger scale. A clean, bright floor tells customers the place is cared for, and a sealed floor is far quicker to clean at closing time.

Heat

Summer highs here regularly pass 110°F, and sun-facing concrete gets hotter still. Heat shortens pot life and can cause bubbles, outgassing and uneven sheen. We work at dawn, keep garage doors closed until the slab is coated, and shade work areas where we can.

Sun

The desert sun yellows standard epoxy over time. Wherever daylight reaches the floor, from open garages to patios, we finish with a UV-stable topcoat, and we steer outdoor colors toward lighter tones that stay cooler.

Wind, dust and monsoon

Spring wind and summer storms push fine dust into every space. We seal openings during application and wipe down before every coat. Monsoon humidity can also slow some products, so we check the forecast before big pours.

Questions we get asked

Can you install epoxy when it is 115 degrees outside?

Not in the afternoon. Hot slabs shorten working time and can make resin cure too fast and bubble. In summer we start at first light, keep batches small, use slower hardeners and finish before the slab heats up. Some projects simply wait for October.

Will hot tires lift the coating?

Hot-tire pickup happens when a coating is too thin, poorly bonded or not fully cured. Grinding, proper film thickness and an honest cure time before parking prevent it. We tell you exactly when it is safe to pull in.

Which coating is best for a garage that faces west?

A west-facing garage takes full afternoon sun. We use a light-stable polyaspartic topcoat over the base so the color does not yellow, and lighter colors that stay cooler underfoot.

Can I coat my RV or boat garage?

Yes. Taller bays often carry heavier loads and more fluids, so we look closely at the slab and joints and use a thicker system where trailer jacks and leveling blocks sit.

Do you coat pool decks and patios?

Yes, with outdoor systems built for sun, water and bare feet. See our patio and concrete coatings page.

How long does a garage take?

Most two- and three-car garages take one to two days of work, plus cure time before vehicles return. Polyaspartic systems shorten that wait.

Do I need to be home during the work?

Not the whole time. We need access, power and a clear space. If you are a winter resident, we can arrange the job around the weeks you are in town.

Are estimates free?

Yes. We come out, look at the concrete and send a written quote. There is no obligation.
